Granted patents and published applications in autonomous vehicle trajectory planning, safety operations, behavioral policy selection, and sensor calibration.

Granted Patents

Granted Waymo LLC

Planning trajectories for controlling autonomous vehicles

Describes a trajectory planning system that evaluates candidate nominal driving paths alongside safety fallback maneuvers—such as safe stopping locations and associated costs—to select smooth, reliable trajectories under dynamic road conditions.

Inventors: Steven Vozar, Ioan-Alexandru Sucan, Jory Denny, Jens-Steffen Ralf Gutmann, Ryan Luna, Jonathan Daudelin, Tian Jin

Granted May Mobility, Inc.

Method and system for dynamically curating autonomous vehicle policies

Details a framework for dynamically selecting and curating behavioral driving policies in real time, enabling an autonomous vehicle to adapt its decision-making set to changing traffic, road geometry, and operational contexts.

Inventors: Steve Vozar, Edwin Olson, Sean M. Messenger, Collin Johnson

Granted May Mobility, Inc.

Method and system for dynamically updating an environmental representation of an autonomous agent

Covers methods for continuously updating an autonomous vehicle’s internal world model by fusing live sensor observations with prior map and scene data to maintain an accurate representation of dynamic surroundings.

Inventors: Steve Vozar, Edwin Olson, Sean M. Messenger, Collin Johnson

US12371067B2
Granted May Mobility, Inc.

Systems and methods for intelligently calibrating infrastructure devices using onboard sensors of an autonomous agent

Uses an autonomous vehicle’s calibrated onboard sensors to automatically localize and align fixed roadside infrastructure sensors—such as smart-intersection cameras or LiDAR—improving vehicle-to-infrastructure perception without manual calibration.

Inventors: Tom Voorheis, Rob Goeddel, Steve Vozar, Edwin Olson

Granted May Mobility, Inc.

Multi-perspective system and method for behavioral policy selection by an autonomous agent

Introduces a multi-policy decision-making architecture that simulates candidate driving behaviors from multiple agent perspectives—accounting for how nearby road users are likely to react—to choose safe, socially aware maneuvers.

Inventors: Steve Vozar, Edwin Olson, et al.
